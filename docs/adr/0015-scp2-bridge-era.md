# ADR 0015: The SCP-2 Bridge Era — from sentence to certified plan

Status: ACCEPTED (era-opening constitution)
Date: 2026-07-05
Author: the director
Governs: SCP2-* packets, until superseded

## Context

The engine's back half is real: 37 registered primitives across eight
capability families, 8 operators as registry citizens behind one
binder, a ledger of 12 compiler-reachable concepts each earned by
correspondence-gated live search, and an aggregation grammar whose
dishonest shapes are unconstructible (ADRs 0013, 0014). But every one
of those 12 concepts reached the ledger through a HAND-AUTHORED
target file, and SCP-1 lowers expressions only to checked-in targets.
A scout cannot ask anything we did not pre-author. The bridge era
closes that: sentence → typed meaning → SYNTHESIZED target → the same
certified search path → moments and honest numbers, or a typed
refusal. The owner's sealed question set is reserved as this era's
final exam.

## Principles

(23) **The meaning expression is the contract.** A typed, versioned,
hash-stable document stating what a question means: primitive and
operator references, constraints, populations, aggregation asks.
Its vocabulary is exactly the knowledge pack — nothing outside the
pack is expressible (attempting it is a typed gap, not an error), and
everything in the pack is expressible. The model side (NL → meaning)
and the machine side (meaning → plan) meet ONLY at this contract;
neither may smuggle semantics past it.

(24) **Synthesis is compilation onto the earned rails, and it never
touches the ledger.** A meaning expression synthesizes a search
target mechanically; the target runs through the SAME
correspondence-gated search that earned the ledger's 12; only
certified plans execute. The synthesized target's semantic
correspondence declaration is derived from the expression itself —
the declaration states the meaning; that is what declarations are
(principle 19) — but ledger rows remain product capability claims
that flip only by the director's hand. Bridge queries ANSWER
questions; they never assert new capability rows.

(25) **Refusal is a first-class outcome, typed, with the smallest
missing thing named.** Exactly three refusal shapes, generalizing
SCL-NL0: clarification_required (ambiguity named as the dimension
needing resolution), understood_but_not_expressible (the smallest
missing capability cited by gap code), unsupported_modality. There is
no fourth outcome and no best-effort guess: a bridge answer is a
certified plan's result or a typed refusal, nothing else. Product
language for refusals follows tri-state law — "cannot express" is
never rendered as "does not exist."

## The era's build order

- **SCP2-1**: meaning-expression schema v0 + the expression→target
  synthesizer (machine side only; no model in the loop). Acceptance:
  held-out expressions synthesize targets whose certified plans
  reproduce known-good answers on the committed tree.
- **SCP2-2**: the model side — NL → meaning expression under the
  SCL-NL0 discipline generalized (same-meaning invariance across
  phrasings, changed-meaning detection, the three refusals), evaluated
  blind on an expanded case set.
- **SCP2-3**: the loop surface — question in, moments + measurements +
  provenance out, pre-warmed, demonstrable cold to an outsider.
- **SCP2-E**: the sealed exam. The owner's reserved question set runs
  blind; the era closes on its results, reported with tri-state
  honesty (answered / refused-correctly / failed).

Operators keep widening the grammar in parallel (sequence_pattern is
next among them — most of the sealed set's question families are
ordered patterns); each new operator enlarges what expressions can
mean without touching the bridge's contract.

## Inherited law

All of ADR 0013 and 0014. The standing bar, the mutation standard,
stage-committed reports, builder-never-judges, and the deviation law
restated at R2-2: when a spec cannot build as written, flag and
request ratification — silently building the right thing is still
building an unratified thing.
